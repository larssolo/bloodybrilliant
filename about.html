ABOUT

A little bit Nordic. A lot unreal.

Bloody Brilliant is a free, endless interactive story that you scroll through in the browser. It was designed and coded by Lars Sohl, the one person behind the studio KodeKid.

What is Bloody Brilliant?

Bloody Brilliant: The Unreal of North is a scroll-driven interactive story in nine chapters. Scrolling drives the story forward (and rewinds it, sound included), and almost everything on screen reacts to a click, a tap or the cursor. It starts as a pastel fairy tale in the north. In grown-up mode it turns dark through a raven of ink, the Norse gods and Ragnarök, then continues sideways into a world of rainbow horses and ends in a multiverse of nonsense. Scroll past the end and the world is reborn with a new seed. The story never ends.

The nine chapters

  1. The Unreal NorthDay turns to sunset and northern lights. Mountains with faces, a shy troll, a Viking balloon, the rainbow bridge Bifrost, a sea serpent and a moose with blooming antlers.
  2. The Singing SkyWarp stars, aurora that bends towards you, a whale made of constellations that sings, and jellyfish.
  3. Lantern WoodsA parallax forest of glowing mushrooms and swinging lanterns. The lights go out one by one and eyes open in the dark.
  4. Sunfall GROWN-UPSThe sun crashes, the sky burns amber and it rains ink.
  5. Bloody Brilliant GROWN-UPSA raven of ink dives down and dissolves into smoke with a burning edge.
  6. Ragnarök GROWN-UPSYggdrasil, burning runes, Odin with one red eye, Huginn and Muninn, and Fenrir breaking free to swallow the world.
  7. The Ink GROWN-UPSInk pours from the wolf’s jaws and the eye of Odin opens and follows you.
  8. PrismA sideways world of Dalí-like horses on stilts, floating islands and rainbow waterfalls. Drag the horses, and paint the world with colour.
  9. The Multiverse of NonsenseTwenty-one figures escape a vector drawing and live by their own physics, until everything is pulled into a vortex and reborn.

For kids, and for grown-ups

Kids mode is the default. A rainbow portal at the end of Lantern Woods leads straight to the horses, so the dark chapters are skipped and never even downloaded. Grown-ups unlock them with a small parental gate in the header or the menu.

How it is made

The whole experience is a single HTML file with no build step and no dependencies apart from Google Fonts. One 2D canvas draws every chapter, and only the visible chapters are drawn. The ink raven is rendered with a small WebGL shader, with a 2D fallback on slower devices. The sound effects are synthesised live with the Web Audio API and play backwards when you scroll backwards. Each chapter has its own AI-composed music loop. A fine animated film grain and a soft vignette make it look printed rather than rendered.

Questions

What is Bloody Brilliant?

A free, scroll-driven interactive story in nine chapters that runs in the browser. It starts as a pastel Nordic fairy tale, can grow dark through Norse mythology and Ragnarök, moves sideways into a world of rainbow horses and ends in a multiverse of nonsense. Then the world is reborn and the story begins again.

Who made it?

Lars Sohl, founder of the one-person studio KodeKid (kodekid.app), designed and coded it.

Is it suitable for children?

Yes. Kids mode is the default. It skips the four dark chapters (Sunfall, Bloody Brilliant, Ragnarök and The Ink) through a rainbow portal, and in the multiverse the tank and soldiers only fire confetti and soap bubbles. Grown-ups can unlock the dark chapters behind a small parental gate.

Is it free, and do I need to install anything?

It is free and runs in any modern web browser on desktop, tablet and phone. There is nothing to install and no account.

How is it built?

A single HTML file with no build step and no dependencies. One 2D canvas draws every chapter, a small WebGL shader renders the ink raven, and the sound effects are synthesised live with the Web Audio API. Each chapter has its own AI-composed music loop.
